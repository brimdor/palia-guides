The Cross-Plot Discovery

Garden Soil tiles snap together to form one continuous block of farmland on your housing plot. This means crops on the edge of one plot are adjacent to crops on the edge of the neighboring plot — and they give/receive buffs exactly like within-plot adjacency.

🌾 Corn on Plot 2's left edge gives Harvest Boost to Plot 1's Apple Tree
💧 Tomato on Plot 4's top edge gives Water Retain to Plot 1's Apple Tree
🌾 Plot 5's Corn corners buff Plot 2/4/6/8's inner crops
  • Apple trees need 3 adjacent crops of the same buff type around their perimeter to activate that buff
  • Blueberry bushes need 2 adjacent crops of the same buff type
  • The 3 adjacent crops can come from multiple neighboring plots — they don't all need to be on the same side
  • A corner apple tree touches 2 neighboring plots (6 exposed perimeter tiles)
  • An edge plot touches 3 neighbors; the center plot touches 4 neighbors

Per-Plot Design & Cross-Plot Effects

Plots 1, 3, 7 — Apple Trees (3×3)
Each apple tree is surrounded by 4 Corn + 2 Tomato from neighboring plots. Since apple trees need only 3 adjacent crops of the same buff type, both Harvest Boost (from 4 corn) and Water Retain (from 2 tomato... wait, only 2 tomato, not 3). Let me recheck.

⚠️ Correction: Plot 2's left column is Corn-Tomato-Corn = 2 Corn + 1 Tomato. Plot 4's top row is Corn-Tomato-Corn = 2 Corn + 1 Tomato. Total touching Plot 1's apple: 4 Corn + 2 Tomato. Harvest Boost activates (needs 3 corn — we have 4). Water Retain does NOT activate for the apple tree (needs 3 tomato — we only have 2). Apple trees get Harvest Boost only.

Plots 2, 4, 6, 8 — Corn-Tomato Checkerboard
5 Corn + 4 Tomato arranged so corn sits on shared edges. Within each plot, every crop gets at least one buff: corn gets Water Retain from adjacent tomatoes; tomatoes get Harvest Boost from adjacent corn.

Plot 5 — Cotton Hub
Center cotton receives Harvest Boost from 4 Corn corners AND Water Retain from 4 Tomato edges. The cotton gives Quality Boost back to all 8 surrounding tiles (more Star Quality corn and tomatoes).

Plot 9 — Blueberry + Corn + Tomato
Blueberry placed top-left (2×2). Surrounded by 2 Corn + 2 Tomato on its exposed edges. Harvest Boost activates from 2 corn. Water Retain from 2 tomato means less watering for the bush.

Processing Priority

This layout produces ~9 apples, ~6 blueberries, ~72 tomatoes, ~54 corn, ~3 cotton every 10 days. You need enough preserve jars to process the queue.

  1. Apple Jam — 144g/jar. ~9 apples every 10 days = 9 runs.
  2. Blueberry Jam — 88g/jar. ~6 berries = 6 runs.
  3. Pickled Tomatoes — 51g/jar. ~72 tomatoes = 72 runs.
  4. Pickled Corn — 60g/jar. ~54 corn = 54 runs.
  5. Seed Maker: Cotton — ~3 cotton = 3 runs.
⚠️ Bottleneck: ~141 preserve-jar items + 3 seed maker items every 10 days. You need ~12–14 preserve jars running continuously. If you have fewer, sell excess tomatoes and corn raw.

Watering Schedule
  • Apple Trees: Minimal watering. Trees are drought-tolerant after initial plant.
  • Checkerboard Plots: Every corn tile is adjacent to at least 1 tomato = water every 2 days instead of daily for most tiles.
  • Plot 5 Cotton Hub: Center cotton receives Water Retain from 4 tomato neighbors + cross-plot tomatoes from all 4 sides = virtually self-watering.
  • Plot 9 Blueberry: Receives Water Retain from 2 adjacent tomatoes = water every 2 days.
⏰ Time Saver: Because Water Retain propagates across plot boundaries, you can water in a spiral starting from any tomato and the moisture will carry to neighbors. Apple trees at the corners need almost no attention.
